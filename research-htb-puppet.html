01 / ENGAGEMENT OVERVIEW

Learning through an interconnected environment.

Puppet is a Hack The Box Mini Pro Lab focused on red-team operations in a simulated organization. The exercise provided an opportunity to practice systematic discovery, maintain an audit trail of observations, and validate findings across different operating systems and security contexts.

My approach emphasized confirming what a tool actually accomplished rather than accepting a success message at face value. Each meaningful milestone was checked independently, and unexpected results were recorded as part of the investigation—not hidden from the final report.

This page intentionally avoids the challenge's attack sequence, commands, host-specific weaknesses, credentials, and flags. Those details are retained in private study notes in line with HTB publication rules.

02 / RESEARCH APPROACH

Four habits that made the difference.

The focus was on repeatable technical reasoning rather than one-off commands.

01 — BASELINE

Understand the scope

Map the permitted environment and document the starting execution context before taking action.

02 — OBSERVE

Capture evidence

Record process identity, privilege levels, system configuration, and tool outputs as testable observations.

03 — VERIFY

Challenge assumptions

Distinguish an API's reported result from the security outcome it is supposed to demonstrate.

04 — DOCUMENT

Preserve the chain

Separate confirmed results from hypotheses and maintain clear notes for each completed objective.

03 / TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT

What this lab reinforced.

Context matters more than a username.

Understanding the effective execution context, token privileges, and operating-system boundaries is essential to evaluating access accurately.

Success messages are not proof.

A reported successful operation is a lead. Independent verification provides the evidence needed for a defensible conclusion.

Cross-platform systems increase complexity.

Windows, Linux, identity services, and automation systems require a consistent method for assessing trust and operational boundaries.

Unexpected results are still findings.

Failed attempts, inconsistent states, and diagnostic logs are valuable when their limitations are recorded clearly.

04 / CLOSING NOTES

Completed. Verified. Documented.
