Packet Capture to Executable Analysis

Recovered a binary from network-capture data, analyzed its runtime behavior, and reconstructed a cryptographic transformation.

Network Forensics / Linux ELFCompleted interview exerciseThird-party challenge
Publication context. This article summarizes an independently completed technical interview exercise using original commentary. The challenge was provided by a third party; the exercise is not represented as my own product, a public vulnerability, or a commissioned professional engagement. Original company branding, challenge assets, flags, and step-by-step solution details have been omitted.

The problem

The starting evidence was packet-capture data containing material associated with a Linux executable. After restoration, the program exited unexpectedly, and its encrypted output could not be understood from network traffic alone.

Research methodology

  1. Inspected captured conversations and reconstructed an executable artifact from the relevant raw bytes.
  2. Used GDB and Ghidra to investigate startup behavior, failure conditions, and the role of encrypted data.
  3. Examined socket-driven behavior in a controlled environment and used a small C implementation to validate the decryption workflow.

Tools & techniques

WiresharkHex editorGDBGhidraNcatC / OpenSSL

Result

The original assessment concluded with a successful decoding of the protected challenge output. The write-up does not establish a security defect in a real-world networked product.

What I practiced

Packet reconstruction, ELF analysis, debugging unexpected process termination, protocol reasoning, and cryptographic implementation testing.

A summary of a completed controlled assessment, not a disclosure of a third-party vulnerability. Results refer only to the exercise materials described.
